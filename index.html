HOW TO PLAY

Don't be the Donkey.

A classic Kerala card game, played in homes, clubs and chaayakadas for generations. Five players, one deck, and a single loser left holding the cards.

01

Follow the suit

The Ace of Spades starts. Everyone plays the suit of the first card. If all follow, the cards are thrown away.

02

Strike!

No card of that suit? Play any other. The round stops, and the highest card of the suit picks up the whole pile.

03

Empty your hand

Get rid of every card to go safe. The last player still holding cards is the Donkey.

THE REGULARS

Twenty characters. Zero mercy.

Every table has its own four regulars, and every one of them plays differently. Pick a table and meet who you’re up against.

Appu

The Ringleader

Loud, cheeky and always first to point at the loser.

“Loser does my homework!”

Ammu

The Unbeatable

Arms crossed, chin up. She has never lost a game. Just ask her.

“I never lose at cards.”

Unni

The Giggler

Laughs at everyone’s bad luck, including his own.

“No crying this time, okay?”

Malu

Ammachi’s Favourite

Sharp, proud, and quick to tell you who taught her.

“Ammachi says I’m the best.”

Shyam

The Carrom Champ

Fresh off a carrom win and still warmed up.

“Carrom’s done. Cards now.”

Faisal

The Club Secretary

Keeps the club rules, and the tea money.

“Club rule: loser buys tea.”

Tomy

The Practiser

Spent all week shuffling, and wants you to know it.

“I’ve been practising, machane.”

Prasad

The Senior

Glasses on, eyebrow up, no patience for new boys.

“Try not to cry, new boy.”

Gopalan

The Chai Lover

Never deals before his next glass of tea.

“One more chai, then we deal.”

Ummer

The Veteran

Forty years at this table. He has seen every trick.

“Forty years I’ve played here.”

Jose

Reads every page, but the game always comes first.

Chandran

The Warner

“Watch your spades, mone.”

Velu

“Under this tree, I’m the king.”

Mathai

The Host

“Sit. The shade is free.”

Raghavan

Remembers every card that has been played.

Sulaiman

Folds his hands, waits, and wins.

“Patience wins. Always.”

The Life of the Shop

Always calling for another round of kallu.

The Watcher

Counts the notes and makes sure the loser pays.
